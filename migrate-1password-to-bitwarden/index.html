🗺️ Migration Steps

1
Export Data from 1Password
Log into your 1Password account via the web vault, as the desktop applications often restrict full exports for security reasons. Navigate to Settings, select Export Data, enter your master password to authorize the action, and download your vault as a .1pux or encrypted CSV file. Ensure you securely delete this exported file from your local machine immediately after the migration is complete.
2
Set Up Your Bitwarden Account
Create a new Bitwarden account or log into your existing organizational vault via the web vault interface. Take time to configure your account security settings, including enabling two-factor authentication via hardware keys or authenticator apps, and setting up your master password hint. If you are managing a team, ensure your Organization is properly established with the appropriate subscription tier before importing data.
3
Import Data into Bitwarden
Inside the Bitwarden web vault, go to Tools and select the Import Data option. Choose 1Password ((.1pux) or CSV) from the format dropdown menu, upload the file you exported earlier, and click Import. Bitwarden will parse your items, logins, and secure notes, automatically routing them into your default vault or selected organization collection.
4
Reorganize Folders and Collections
Because 1Password tags and vaults do not always map identically to Bitwarden folders and collections, review your imported items for accuracy. Move shared credentials into appropriate Bitwarden Organization collections and assign user access permissions. Tag unorganized items and verify that custom fields and secure notes transferred over correctly without truncation.
5
Onboard Team Members
If you are migrating a business or family plan, invite your users to Bitwarden via email from the Organization management console. Instruct team members to accept their invitations, set up their own master passwords, and join the shared collections you have prepared. Distribute any emergency access or admin roles according to your organization operational policies.
6
Complete the Cutover and Uninstall 1Password
Install the Bitwarden browser extensions and native desktop and mobile applications on all your devices. Test autofill functionality and emergency login recovery to ensure everything works seamlessly before revoking your 1Password subscription. Finally, securely empty your local trash bins and uninstall 1Password apps from your operating systems.

⚠️ Common Challenges & How to Avoid Them

1Password document attachments and file storage do not always map cleanly during a standard CSV or .1pux import.
Manually download critical file attachments from your 1Password vault prior to export and re-upload them directly to the corresponding secure notes or items in Bitwarden.
1Password handles multiple URLs and custom fields differently than Bitwarden, which can occasionally break autofill scripts on complex login pages.
Review imported items in Bitwarden with multiple URI fields and adjust the match detection settings (such as exact, base domain, or host) to ensure reliable autofill behavior.
Shared vaults and nested folder hierarchies from 1Password flatten out into flat folders or collections upon import.
Use Bitwarden Collections for team separation and manually recreate any nested folder structures within your personal or organizational vault post-import.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Will my 1Password Travel Mode settings or secure attachments transfer over automatically?
Standard text data, logins, and secure notes transfer smoothly, but 1Password-specific features like Travel Mode do not exist in Bitwarden. File attachments may need to be re-uploaded manually depending on the export format you choose.
Is Bitwarden really more cost-effective than 1Password for families and teams?
Yes. Bitwarden offers a robust free tier and significantly lower pricing for premium individual, family, and enterprise plans compared to 1Password standard subscription costs.
Can I self-host Bitwarden after migrating from 1Password's cloud service?
Yes, Bitwarden offers official Docker containers that allow you to host your own password manager server on local hardware or private cloud infrastructure, a feature 1Password does not support.
