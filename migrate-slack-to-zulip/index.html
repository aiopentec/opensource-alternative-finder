📦 Migration Guide

How to Migrate from Slack to Zulip

💰 Save $7.25-$15/user/month/month 🔓 Own your data 🖥️ Setup: Moderate (~1-2 hours) 📅 August 23, 2026

Migrating from Slack to Zulip is a strategic move for organizations looking to escape Slack's escalating per-user pricing, message history paywalls, and chaotic unstructured chat. While Slack relies on a flat channel structure that easily devolves into noise and overlapping conversations, Zulip introduces a unique topic-based threading model. In Zulip, every conversation lives within a stream and is organized by a specific topic, which fundamentally transforms how teams communicate asynchronously without losing context.

Organizations typically make this switch when their Slack bill becomes unsustainable—often paying between $7.25 and $15 per user monthly—or when they require the robust data ownership and cost control of an open-source platform. By self-hosting Zulip or using its managed cloud service, teams gain unlimited message history and predictable expenses. However, moving from Slack's channel paradigm to Zulip's stream-and-topic architecture requires careful planning to ensure your team adapts successfully to a more organized, less chaotic way of working.

🗺️ Migration Steps

1
Audit and Clean Up Your Slack Workspace
Before exporting, review your existing Slack channels, direct messages, and integrations to decide what is worth keeping. Archive obsolete channels and inactive bots to reduce clutter and streamline the migration process into your new Zulip organization.
2
Export Data from Slack
Workspace owners can export Slack data from the Workspace Settings menu by navigating to Import/Export and selecting Export. Note that Slack's standard export tool provides JSON files for public channels, while private channels and direct messages require higher-tier plans or specialized compliance exports.
3
Set Up Your Zulip Organization
Create a new Zulip organization either by spinning up a self-hosted server via Docker or by registering on Zulip Cloud. Configure your base settings, user authentication methods like Google or SAML, and initial permissions before bringing in any data.
4
Import Slack Data into Zulip
Use Zulip's built-in migration tool specifically designed to import Slack export archives. This script maps Slack users, public channels, and message histories into Zulip streams, preserving as much historical context as possible while adapting to Zulip's data structures.
5
Onboard Your Team and Explain Topics
Invite your team members to the new Zulip instance and guide them through the interface. Emphasize the importance of Zulip's topic-based threading, as Slack users are accustomed to replying in threads that vanish into sidebars rather than organizing conversations by distinct topic headers.
6
Execute the Final Cutover
Set a hard deadline to stop using Slack and officially switch your daily operations to Zulip. Disable your Slack workspace integrations, update company documentation with the new Zulip URL, and monitor user adoption closely during the first week.

⚠️ Common Challenges & How to Avoid Them

Slack users struggle to adapt to Zulip's topic-based threading model instead of flat channel feeds.
Conduct a mandatory 15-minute training session demonstrating how topics keep conversations organized and prevent cross-talk in busy streams.
Standard Slack exports omit private channel history and direct message archives unless you have a paid enterprise plan.
Communicate to your team in advance that historical DMs will not transfer over, and encourage them to save critical notes before the cutover.
Custom Slack integrations and slash commands break because they are not natively compatible with Zulip's API.
Review your active Slack app directory and replace essential tools with Zulip's built-in integrations or configure webhook equivalents.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Can I import my message history from Slack into Zulip?
Yes, Zulip provides official migration scripts that import public channel archives from a standard Slack export. The tool automatically maps users and preserves historical conversations within Zulip's streams, though private messages require enterprise-tier Slack exports.
How does Zulip's pricing compare to Slack?
Slack charges between $7.25 and $15 per user per month with message history paywalls on free tiers. In contrast, Zulip is open-source and free to self-host indefinitely, and its managed cloud hosting is significantly more affordable than Slack's comparable enterprise pricing.
What is the biggest learning curve for users switching from Slack to Zulip?
The main adjustment is learning to use Zulip's mandatory topic fields for every message sent to a stream. While Slack users are used to a single continuous chat feed, Zulip's topic system requires naming the subject of discussion, which drastically improves asynchronous readability.
