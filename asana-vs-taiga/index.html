Asana vs Taiga (2026)

Overview

Asana and Taiga are project management platforms designed to track tasks, coordinate teams, and manage workflows, but they cater to fundamentally different deployment and operational philosophies. Project managers, IT directors, and engineering leads evaluate these tools when deciding between a hosted, proprietary SaaS application and an open-source, self-hosted alternative. Teams typically switch from Asana to Taiga to eliminate per-seat licensing costs, gain complete data ownership, or align their project management infrastructure with strict internal compliance policies.

Key Differences

  • Cost: Asana operates on a tiered SaaS subscription model with per-user monthly fees, whereas Taiga is completely free and open-source when self-hosted, with paid cloud options available for smaller setups.
  • Data Ownership: Asana stores all data on its proprietary cloud servers, while Taiga allows full self-hosting via Docker, giving organizations total control over their data residency and security posture.
  • Setup Complexity: Asana requires zero infrastructure setup with immediate cloud availability, whereas Taiga demands technical proficiency to deploy, configure, and maintain containerized instances and database backups.
  • Scalability: Asana scales automatically to tens of thousands of users with enterprise-grade uptime SLAs, while Taiga's scalability is constrained entirely by the hardware resources and administrative overhead of the host organization.
  • Feature Focus: Asana emphasizes cross-functional business workflows with native enterprise integrations like Salesforce and Tableau, while Taiga focuses heavily on agile software development with native Scrum and Kanban modules, backlogs, and issue tracking.

Pros and Cons

Asana

  • Pros:
- Instant cloud deployment with zero infrastructure management required. - Extensive library of native enterprise integrations (Slack, Salesforce, Jira). - Polished, intuitive user interface suitable for non-technical departments. - Advanced reporting tools like Universal Workload and custom dashboards.
  • Cons:
- Costs scale rapidly as team size and feature requirements increase. - Vendor lock-in with proprietary data formats and closed infrastructure. - Limited customization for agile-specific workflows compared to developer tools. - Enterprise features locked behind expensive tier upgrades.

Taiga

  • Pros:
- 100% free and open-source under the Mozilla Public License 2.0. - Complete data ownership and control through on-premise self-hosting. - Excellent native support for agile methodologies (Scrum, Kanban, backlogs). - Highly extensible architecture with active community plugin support.
  • Cons:
- Requires dedicated DevOps resources for initial setup, maintenance, and updates. - Smaller third-party integration ecosystem than market-leading SaaS tools. - Limited administrative reporting out of the box for non-technical stakeholders. - Documentation for complex self-hosted scaling scenarios can be sparse.

When to Choose Each

Asana

Choose Asana if you manage cross-functional teams spanning marketing, sales, and operations who require an out-of-the-box SaaS solution with minimal IT overhead. It fits mid-to-large enterprises that rely heavily on third-party SaaS integrations, require guaranteed uptime SLAs, and have budgets to support per-seat licensing fees without internal infrastructure maintenance costs.

Taiga

Choose Taiga if you run software development teams of 10 to 500+ members who prioritize agile workflows, open-source software, and strict data sovereignty. It suits organizations with dedicated IT or DevOps personnel who want to avoid recurring per-user licensing fees by hosting their project management infrastructure on internal hardware or private clouds.

Migration Path

  1. Export your Asana projects and tasks using Asana's native JSON or CSV export functionality from the project settings menu.
  2. Provision a production server and deploy Taiga using the official Docker Compose installation guide.
  3. Transform your exported Asana CSV or JSON data to match Taiga's import schema using custom scripts or community-maintained migration utilities.
  4. Import the formatted data into your running Taiga instance via the administration panel or API endpoints.
  5. Verify user accounts, assign team permissions, and run test sprints to ensure project history and task statuses transferred correctly.

Our Take

Taiga is production-ready for tech-savvy organizations, engineering teams, and privacy-conscious enterprises that possess the internal DevOps capability to manage Docker-based deployments. However, it is not a drop-in plug-and-play replacement for non-technical business units accustomed to polished consumer SaaS applications. One specific technical limitation worth knowing before switching is Taiga's reliance on community-driven or custom scripts for data migration from Asana, as there is no official, click-to-run Asana importer built into the core platform.

The recommendation is clear-cut based on your operational profile. Teams with dedicated IT staff looking to escape escalating per-seat SaaS costs and secure their data on-premise should switch to Taiga immediately. Conversely, organizations dependent on turnkey enterprise integrations, immediate cloud reliability, and non-technical end-users should stay with Asana.

Who Should Switch

  • Engineering teams of 20 to 100 developers who do not need proprietary SaaS business intelligence tools and want native Scrum/Kanban backlogs.
  • Privacy-regulated organizations with 50+ users that do not need cloud-based vendor dependencies and require complete on-premise data residency.
  • Cost-conscious startups of 15 to 50 employees who do not need Salesforce integrations and want to eliminate recurring per-user software licensing overhead.
  • Open-source advocates and Linux system administration teams of any size who do not need commercial customer support and prefer self-managed infrastructure.
  • Development agencies managing client projects internally who do not need Asana's marketing-focused reporting suites and prefer isolated Docker environments.

FAQ

Can I import my Asana tasks directly into Taiga using an official migration tool?

No, Taiga does not currently offer a native, one-click importer for Asana data. Migrating requires exporting your Asana tasks to CSV or JSON and writing custom transformation scripts to map them into Taiga's database schema.

Is Taiga entirely free to use?

Taiga is completely free and open-source under the MPL 2.0 license when you self-host it on your own infrastructure. They also offer a paid cloud-hosted SaaS version if you prefer not to manage your own servers.

How does Taiga handle user permissions compared to Asana?

Taiga provides robust project-level role-based access control, allowing administrators to define granular permissions for members, stakeholders, and guests. Asana handles permissions similarly through team and project membership tiers, though its enterprise controls are tied to higher pricing plans.

Does Taiga support time tracking out of the box?

Yes, Taiga includes built-in time tracking features that allow team members to log hours against specific tasks and user stories. This matches functionality found in Asana's paid tiers without requiring external third-party integrations.

Can I run Taiga on Kubernetes?

Yes, the open-source community maintains reliable Helm charts and manifests for deploying Taiga on Kubernetes clusters. This makes it suitable for enterprises standardizing their infrastructure on container orchestration platforms.
