Jira vs Plane (2026)

Overview

Jira and Plane are two project management tools designed to help teams organize and track their work. This comparison is beneficial for teams currently using Jira and considering switching to Plane, likely due to Plane's self-hosted option and free cost. The core use case for both tools is issue tracking and project management, and the key reason someone would switch is to gain more control over their data and reduce costs.

Key Differences

  • Cost: Jira charges a per-user monthly fee, while Plane is free and self-hosted.
  • Data ownership: Plane allows teams to host their own data, while Jira stores data on its servers.
  • Setup complexity: Jira has a more straightforward setup process, while Plane requires more technical expertise to self-host.
  • Scalability: Jira is designed to handle large teams and enterprises, while Plane is better suited for smaller teams and organizations.
  • Integration with Git: Jira has native integration with Git, while Plane requires a third-party plugin to achieve similar functionality.

Pros and Cons

Jira

  • Pros:
+ Easy to set up and use + Native integration with Git + Scalable for large teams and enterprises + Wide range of third-party integrations available + Strong customer support
  • Cons:
+ Expensive, especially for large teams + Limited control over data storage and security + Proprietary license

Plane

  • Pros:
+ Free and self-hosted + High degree of control over data storage and security + Customizable and extensible + Apache 2.0 license + Cost-effective for small to medium-sized teams
  • Cons:
+ Requires technical expertise to set up and maintain + Limited native integrations with other tools + May not be suitable for very large teams or enterprises + Limited customer support

When to Choose Each

Jira is a good choice for large teams and enterprises that need a scalable and easy-to-use project management tool, and are willing to pay a premium for it. Plane, on the other hand, is a good choice for small to medium-sized teams that want a high degree of control over their data and are comfortable with self-hosting and maintaining their own software.

Migration Path

  1. Export your Jira data in CSV or JSON format.
  2. Set up a Plane instance on your own server.
  3. Use the Plane import tool to import your Jira data.
  4. Configure any necessary plugins or integrations in Plane.
  5. Test your Plane instance to ensure everything is working as expected.

Our Take

Plane is not yet production-ready for most users, as it still lacks some of the polish and features of Jira. One specific technical limitation worth knowing is that Plane's API is still in development, which may limit its integrations with other tools. However, for small to medium-sized teams that value control over their data and are comfortable with self-hosting, Plane is a clear winner, and we recommend switching from Jira to take advantage of its cost-effectiveness and customizability.

Who Should Switch

  • Small teams (less than 10 users) that don't need native Git integration
  • Medium-sized teams (10-50 users) that want more control over their data
  • Teams that are currently using Jira's free plan and are outgrowing it
  • Teams that are comfortable with self-hosting and maintaining their own software
  • Teams that prioritize cost-effectiveness and customizability over ease of use

FAQ

Can I use Plane for free?

Yes, Plane is free and self-hosted, which means you can use it at no cost beyond the cost of hosting your own server.

Is Plane as scalable as Jira?

No, Plane is not yet as scalable as Jira, and may not be suitable for very large teams or enterprises.

Can I integrate Plane with other tools?

Yes, Plane has a growing ecosystem of plugins and integrations, but it may not have the same level of native integration as Jira.

How do I migrate my data from Jira to Plane?

You can export your Jira data in CSV or JSON format and then use the Plane import tool to import it.

Is Plane's API fully developed?

Not yet, Plane's API is still in development, which may limit its integrations with other tools.
