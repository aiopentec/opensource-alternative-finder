How to Migrate from Zoom to Jitsi Meet

💰 Save Free / $13.33+/user/month/month 🔓 Own your data 🖥️ Setup: Easy (~30 mins) 📅 August 30, 2026

Migrating from Zoom to Jitsi Meet is an attractive move for organizations looking to eliminate recurring licensing costs and retain absolute control over their communication infrastructure. While Zoom charges upwards of $13.33 per user per month for business tiers and enforces 40-minute limits on free group sessions, Jitsi Meet is a completely free, open-source WebRTC video conferencing solution. Teams choose to migrate when they want to avoid vendor lock-in, eliminate per-user subscription fees, and ensure compliance with strict internal data privacy mandates by hosting meetings on their own servers.

Transitioning away from Zoom requires a shift in mindset, moving from a proprietary, account-heavy ecosystem to an instant, browser-based architecture. Zoom relies heavily on downloadable desktop clients, personal meeting IDs, and centralized user management dashboards. Jitsi Meet operates primarily out of standard web browsers without mandatory client downloads or forced user registration. This migration guide outlines how to successfully transition your organization from Zoom proprietary infrastructure to a flexible, open-source Jitsi Meet deployment.

🗺️ Migration Steps

1
Audit Current Zoom Usage and Licensing
Begin by reviewing your active Zoom subscriptions, user seat counts, and recurring monthly invoices to calculate your potential financial savings. Identify which teams rely on advanced Zoom features like automated cloud recording, large webinar licenses, or native telephony dial-in integration. Document your existing recurring meeting schedules and calendar integrations so you can replicate them in your new environment.
2
Choose Your Jitsi Deployment Model
Decide whether your organization will use the free public community instance at meet.jit.si or deploy a private, self-hosted Jitsi Meet server on your own cloud infrastructure. Self-hosting requires provisioning a Linux server using Docker or Debian packages, configuring SSL certificates, and opening specific UDP ports for media traffic. For most migrating businesses, setting up a private instance ensures maximum data sovereignty and customization.
3
Configure Authentication and Security Settings
Unlike Zoom where hosts manage accounts centrally, Jitsi Meet allows for anonymous participation by default. If your organization requires secure access control, integrate your Jitsi server with an identity provider like LDAP, Keycloak, or JWT authentication. This ensures that only authorized team members can act as moderators and start scheduled company meetings.
4
Migrate Calendar Integrations and Links
Zoom users typically rely on the Zoom Outlook and Google Calendar plugins to auto-generate meeting links. Install the official Jitsi Meet browser extensions and calendar plugins for Google Workspace and Microsoft 365 to replace your existing workflows. Update your team email templates and default calendar invite text to automatically generate unique Jitsi room URLs instead of Zoom links.
5
Conduct Team Onboarding and Training
Host internal workshop sessions to familiarize your staff with the Jitsi Meet interface, browser permissions, and moderator controls. Teach users how to lock rooms with a password, manage screen sharing, and utilize the built-in chat feature without relying on Zoom-specific nomenclature. Emphasize the convenience of joining meetings directly via a web browser link without needing to install proprietary desktop software.
6
Execute the Final Cutover
Set a definitive date to cancel your paid Zoom enterprise contracts and transition all internal and external communication to Jitsi Meet. Remove Zoom desktop client deployment scripts from IT management tools and replace them with Jitsi browser shortcuts. Monitor server performance and audio-video quality closely during the first week to ensure your infrastructure handles the new traffic load.

⚠️ Common Challenges & How to Avoid Them

Jitsi Meet lacks Zoom native cloud recording out of the box, requiring local recording solutions.
Configure Jitsi Videobridge to integrate with Jigasi and Jibri to enable server-side recording to Dropbox or local storage, or train users to record directly through their browser.
Self-hosted Jitsi servers can experience poor video quality or packet loss if server resources are undersized.
Provision a cloud instance with adequate CPU and RAM, and ensure your server firewall correctly routes UDP media traffic through the recommended ports.
Users accustomed to Zoom breakout rooms may struggle with Jitsi lack of a native, single-click breakout feature.
Advise teams to create separate, dedicated Jitsi room URLs in advance for different working groups and instruct participants to switch links manually when needed.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Is Jitsi Meet truly free compared to Zoom paid plans?
Yes, Jitsi Meet is 100 percent free and open-source software with no per-user licensing fees or time limits on meetings. If you self-host on a cloud server, your only cost is the underlying infrastructure hosting fee, which is typically a fraction of Zoom pricing.
Do meeting participants need to install an app to join a Jitsi Meet call?
No, participants can join Jitsi Meet entirely through a web browser without downloading any software or creating an account. Mobile users can download the optional iOS or Android app, but desktop users only need to click the shared room link.
How secure are meetings on Jitsi Meet compared to Zoom?
Jitsi Meet offers robust security including encrypted WebRTC media streams, optional room passwords, and moderator controls. When self-hosted, your organization retains complete ownership of all meeting metadata and data streams, eliminating third-party data harvesting.
