How to Migrate from Hotjar to OpenReplay

💰 Save Free / $32-$80+/month/month 🔓 Own your data 📅 August 27, 2026

Migrating from Hotjar to OpenReplay is a strategic move for organizations looking to transition from a proprietary, usage-tiered SaaS product to a self-hosted, open-source session replay and product analytics suite. While Hotjar excels at quick deployments and general feedback collection, its pricing model scales aggressively with high traffic volumes, and cloud-hosted data governance can conflict with strict enterprise privacy requirements like GDPR or HIPAA. OpenReplay gives you full ownership of your data by running on your own infrastructure, offering unlimited session recording, advanced developer-centric debugging tools, and integrated error tracking without unexpected cost surges.

Teams typically initiate this migration when their monthly Hotjar bill becomes prohibitive due to traffic growth, or when they require deeper technical insights such as network payload inspection, console logs, and Redux action monitoring. OpenReplay bridges the gap between traditional product analytics and developer debugging. By moving to OpenReplay, you retain essential user behavior tools like heatmaps and funnels while gaining granular control over backend performance metrics and self-hosted security.

🗺️ Migration Steps

1
Audit existing Hotjar setup and data
Begin by cataloging all active Hotjar features in use, including heatmaps, incoming feedback widgets, surveys, and specific session recording filters. Document your current high-traffic pages and sampling rates so you can configure OpenReplay to match or optimize your data capture requirements. Since Hotjar does not offer a direct data export for historical recordings, accept that past session data will remain in Hotjar until it naturally ages out.
2
Provision and configure OpenReplay infrastructure
Deploy OpenReplay on your own cloud infrastructure using Docker and Kubernetes, ensuring your database and object storage are properly secured and scaled for your expected traffic volume. Configure your organization settings, retention policies, and user permissions within the OpenReplay dashboard to mirror your team workflow. Unlike Hotjar, you will manage your own storage lifecycle, so provision adequate disk space for recorded DOM mutations and network payloads.
3
Replace the Hotjar tracking snippet
Remove the Hotjar tracking code script from your website headers and applications. Install the OpenReplay tracking script or NPM package across your application codebases. Take advantage of OpenReplay's advanced SDK to capture custom events, sanitize sensitive form inputs, and record console logs or Redux actions that Hotjar's standard snippet misses.
4
Configure privacy masking and security rules
Hotjar automatically masks form inputs, but OpenReplay requires explicit configuration for robust data masking to comply with privacy regulations on self-hosted servers. Set up CSS selectors in the OpenReplay initialization script to obscure PII, credit card numbers, and sensitive user data before it leaves the browser. Verify your masking rules in a staging environment to ensure no critical UI elements are accidentally hidden or exposed.
5
Recreate dashboards, funnels, and heatmaps
Rebuild your core conversion funnels, user paths, and click heatmaps inside the OpenReplay dashboard. Because OpenReplay calculates these analytics from the underlying session data, verify that your custom events and page views are firing correctly. Use OpenReplay's segmentation features to group users by custom metadata, replacing Hotjar's user attributes.
6
Perform cutover and team onboarding
Verify incoming session streams in OpenReplay to ensure recordings render smoothly and console logs match expectations. Invite your product managers, UX researchers, and developers to the OpenReplay workspace and conduct a brief orientation on its developer-centric debugging features. Once verified, cancel your Hotjar subscription to complete the migration.

⚠️ Common Challenges & How to Avoid Them

Loss of historical session recordings and feedback data from Hotjar
Export any critical survey results or feedback items to CSV from Hotjar before cancellation, and rely on fresh data accumulation in OpenReplay for ongoing analysis.
Managing self-hosted infrastructure and storage costs for heavy session recording volumes
Implement OpenReplay's ingestion sampling rates and configure object storage lifecycle policies to automatically prune old recordings and manage disk usage effectively.
Developer onboarding for advanced features like network payload inspection and Redux tracking
Schedule a dedicated workshop for your engineering team to demonstrate how OpenReplay correlates frontend user actions with backend network requests and console errors.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Can I export my historical session recordings from Hotjar to OpenReplay?
No, Hotjar does not provide an API or export mechanism for historical session videos. You will start fresh with new recordings collected by the OpenReplay tracking script.
Is OpenReplay really free compared to Hotjar's paid tiers?
OpenReplay offers a free, open-source community edition that you can self-host indefinitely without per-session limits. You only pay for your own server and storage infrastructure, whereas Hotjar charges monthly subscription fees based on daily session volumes.
How does OpenReplay handle data privacy and compliance differently than Hotjar?
While Hotjar processes and stores all data on their cloud servers under GDPR compliance, OpenReplay is self-hosted on your own infrastructure. This gives your organization 100 percent ownership and control over user data, making it ideal for strict enterprise security policies.
