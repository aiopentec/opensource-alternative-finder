📦 Migration Guide

How to Migrate from Shopify to WooCommerce

💰 Save $29-$299+/month/month 🔓 Own your data 📅 August 28, 2026

Migrating from Shopify to WooCommerce is a strategic move for store owners looking to escape rising monthly SaaS fees and transaction surcharges. While Shopify offers a streamlined, walled-garden approach starting at $29 to $299+ per month, its proprietary liquid architecture limits deep backend customization and database-level ownership. WooCommerce, built as a free, open-source plugin for WordPress, grants you complete control over your source code, server environment, and data without proprietary vendor lock-in.

You should consider making the switch when your business hits a growth plateau where Shopify Plus pricing becomes prohibitive, or when your product catalog requires complex, non-standard database relationships and custom checkout workflows. Moving to WooCommerce puts you in the driver's seat of your hosting infrastructure, allowing you to optimize performance, integrate niche APIs, and eliminate monthly platform fees entirely. However, because WooCommerce requires self-hosting, you transition from a hands-off maintenance model to managing your own updates, security hardening, and database optimization.

🗺️ Migration Steps

1
Prepare Your WordPress and WooCommerce Environment
Before touching your Shopify data, secure a reliable web host optimized for WooCommerce and install a fresh copy of WordPress. Install the official WooCommerce plugin along with a compatible theme and essential security plugins to establish your new storefront foundation.
2
Export Data from Shopify
Navigate to your Shopify admin dashboard and export your products, customers, and orders into CSV file formats. For reviews, blog posts, and complex metadata, you may need third-party apps or specialized exporters to capture all historical database entries.
3
Map and Import Data into WooCommerce
Use the built-in WooCommerce Product CSV Importer or a dedicated migration plugin like Cart2Cart to map your Shopify data fields to WooCommerce attributes. Carefully verify that SKUs, product variations, inventory counts, and customer account histories align correctly during the upload.
4
Configure Payment Gateways and Shipping Zones
Reconfigure your checkout experience by setting up payment processors like Stripe, PayPal, or local gateways within WooCommerce. Rebuild your shipping flat rates, weight-based rules, and tax calculations to match your previous Shopify configuration.
5
Train Your Team on the WordPress Dashboard
Familiarize your fulfillment staff and content editors with the WordPress and WooCommerce backend interface. Establish new user roles, permissions, and internal workflows for managing daily orders, inventory adjustments, and customer inquiries.
6
Perform Final Testing and DNS Cutover
Run test transactions using sandbox mode to ensure your checkout, confirmation emails, and inventory deduction work seamlessly. Once verified, update your domain name server records to point your live URL away from Shopify and toward your new WooCommerce host.

⚠️ Common Challenges & How to Avoid Them

Broken SEO URLs and lost organic search traffic due to differing URL structures.
Map your old Shopify URL slugs to your new WordPress permalink structure using 301 redirects via a plugin like Redirection to preserve link equity.
Incompatible Shopify apps that lack direct equivalents in the WordPress plugin ecosystem.
Audit your Shopify app stack prior to migration and find alternative WooCommerce extensions or hire a developer to build custom API integrations.
Managing server resources and performance drops under high traffic spikes.
Invest in a managed WordPress host with robust caching mechanisms, a reliable CDN, and scalable PHP worker limits to handle traffic surges smoothly.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Will my customers lose their passwords when I migrate from Shopify to WooCommerce?
Yes, Shopify encrypts customer passwords in a way that cannot be directly exported or decrypted for security reasons. You will need to prompt your customers to reset their passwords the first time they log into your new WooCommerce store.
How long does a typical Shopify to WooCommerce migration take?
A basic migration with standard products and customers can take anywhere from a few hours to a couple of days. However, complex stores with heavy customizations, custom apps, and extensive SEO redirection mapping may take several weeks.
Is WooCommerce truly free compared to Shopify?
The WooCommerce software itself is free and open-source, but you must pay for third-party hosting, a domain name, SSL certificates, and premium extensions. While it often lowers total operating costs for high-volume stores, it is not a zero-cost solution.
