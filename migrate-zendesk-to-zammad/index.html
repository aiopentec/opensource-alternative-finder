How to Migrate from Zendesk to Zammad

Migrating from Zendesk to Zammad is a strategic move for organizations looking to escape escalating software subscription costs while retaining enterprise-grade ticketing capabilities. Zendesk pricing tiers range from $19 to $115 per user per month, making it a heavy financial burden for growing support teams, whereas Zammad is a powerful open-source helpdesk alternative that offers a free Community Edition alongside affordable hosted options. Organizations typically initiate this migration when subscription renewals loom large or when data sovereignty requirements demand an on-premise solution that keeps customer service logs entirely within private infrastructure.

Transitioning requires careful planning because Zendesk and Zammad handle data models differently. While Zendesk relies heavily on proprietary cloud structures and custom ticket fields, Zammad utilizes a relational database and offers deep integrations with modern communication channels like Telegram, WhatsApp, and Twitter out of the box. Successfully moving your helpdesk means not only migrating historical ticket data and user profiles, but also retraining your support agents on Zammad's efficient, tab-based interface and distinct workflow automation engine.

🗺️ Migration Steps

1
Audit and Export Zendesk Data
Begin by exporting your historical data from Zendesk using their API or built-in JSON and CSV export tools. Carefully review your active macros, triggers, user roles, and custom ticket fields to determine what actually needs to be carried over to your new Zammad instance.
2
Provision and Configure Your Zammad Instance
Set up your Zammad environment, choosing between a self-hosted Linux server for complete data control or Zammad's managed cloud hosting. Configure your organization's core settings, define agent roles, and recreate essential communication channels like support email addresses.
3
Map Zendesk Fields to Zammad Schema
Because Zendesk and Zammad structure ticket metadata and user attributes differently, you must map your exported CSV or JSON fields to Zammad schema requirements. Ensure that requester IDs, agent assignments, and ticket statuses align correctly before running any import scripts.
4
Import Historical Tickets and Users
Utilize Zammad's built-in migration wizard or custom Python scripts interacting with the Zammad REST API to import your users, organizations, and historical tickets. Run test batches first to verify that attachments, internal notes, and timestamps transfer accurately without corruption.
5
Train Support Agents on Zammad Workflows
Introduce your support team to Zammad's unique interface, emphasizing its tabbed browsing, fast search capabilities, and integrated knowledge base. Rebuild your most critical Zendesk automation rules and triggers as Zammad core workflows and automated triggers.
6
Execute DNS Cutover and Go Live
Perform a final delta import to capture any tickets created in Zendesk during the migration window. Update your support email MX records and DNS settings to point incoming customer requests directly to your new Zammad helpdesk, officially concluding the migration.

⚠️ Common Challenges & How to Avoid Them

Zendesk proprietary macros and advanced triggers do not automatically translate into Zammad's automation logic.
Document your existing Zendesk business rules beforehand and manually rebuild them using Zammad's trigger and core workflow engine.
Exporting large volumes of ticket attachments and historical comments via the Zendesk API can hit rate limits or timeout errors.
Use pagination in your extraction scripts, run exports during off-peak hours, and process attachments in smaller, manageable batches.
Support agents accustomed to Zendesk's specific UI layout may experience friction during the initial days on Zammad.
Conduct hands-on training sessions focusing on Zammad's multi-tasking tab interface and global search shortcuts to accelerate user adoption.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Can I migrate historical ticket attachments and internal notes from Zendesk to Zammad?
Yes, attachments and internal notes can be successfully migrated by utilizing custom migration scripts that interface with the Zammad API. You must ensure your script downloads the file binaries from Zendesk and uploads them to the corresponding Zammad ticket objects during the import process.
How does Zammad's total cost of ownership compare to Zendesk?
Zammad offers a free open-source Community Edition where you only pay for your own server hosting, drastically undercutting Zendesk's per-user monthly fees. Even Zammad's paid hosted plans are significantly more cost-effective for teams scaling beyond a handful of agents.
Is it possible to self-host Zammad on our own local servers?
Yes, unlike Zendesk which is strictly a cloud-as-a-service platform, Zammad can be fully deployed on-premise using Docker or standard Linux distributions. This gives your organization complete ownership and control over your customer support database and data privacy compliance.
