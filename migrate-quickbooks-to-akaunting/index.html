📦 Migration Guide

How to Migrate from QuickBooks to Akaunting

💰 Save $30-$200+/month/month 🔓 Own your data 📅 August 31, 2026

Migrating from QuickBooks to Akaunting is a smart financial move for small business owners and freelancers looking to eliminate recurring monthly subscription fees that range from thirty to over two hundred dollars. QuickBooks is a closed, proprietary giant with tiered pricing and frequent forced upgrades, whereas Akaunting is a free, open-source alternative that gives you complete ownership of your financial data through self-hosting or managed cloud options. By making this switch, you retain full functionality for invoicing, expense tracking, and vendor management without the burden of escalating software overhead.

You should consider this migration at the end of a fiscal quarter or calendar year when financial reporting naturally resets, making it cleaner to establish opening balances. Transitioning is ideal if your business requires custom modules from the Akaunting App Store or if you simply want the privacy and security of storing your financial ledger on your own private server. While QuickBooks locks your historical data behind active subscription walls, moving to Akaunting ensures your historical records remain accessible and fully under your control indefinitely.

🗺️ Migration Steps

1
Export Data from QuickBooks Online or Desktop
Log into your QuickBooks account and navigate to the Export Data tool under your settings. You will need to export key reports and lists separately, specifically your Chart of Accounts, Customers, Vendors, and Products or Services into CSV or Excel formats.
2
Install and Configure Akaunting
Set up your Akaunting environment by either deploying the self-hosted open-source package on your own web server or signing up for Akaunting Cloud. Complete the initial wizard by entering your company details, setting your base currency, and configuring tax rates.
3
Clean and Format Your CSV Files
QuickBooks export files often contain proprietary formatting and internal ID columns that Akaunting will not recognize during import. Open your exported CSVs in a spreadsheet editor to clean up headers, align columns with Akaunting import schemas, and remove orphaned data.
4
Import Core Lists and Opening Balances
Utilize Akaunting native data import tools or community modules to upload your cleaned CSV files for customers, vendors, and items. Next, enter your trial balance or account opening balances so your general ledger matches the exact state you left QuickBooks.
5
Onboard Your Team and Accountants
Invite your internal staff and external accountants to your new Akaunting workspace by assigning appropriate user roles and permissions. Walk them through the Akaunting dashboard, which features a streamlined interface that mirrors many standard accounting workflows found in QuickBooks.
6
Perform the Final Cutover
Stop recording new invoices and bills inside QuickBooks to prevent data drift between the two systems. Send out your very first invoice from Akaunting, reconcile your bank feeds, and officially cancel your QuickBooks subscription to start saving money.

⚠️ Common Challenges & How to Avoid Them

QuickBooks uses proprietary file formats (like .QBW for Desktop) that Akaunting cannot read natively.
You must first convert your QuickBooks Desktop files into CSV or IIF formats, or use a third-party migration utility before importing them into Akaunting.
Historical transaction data such as past paid invoices and old journal entries rarely map over cleanly via standard CSV imports.
Import only your active customers, vendors, and current account balances, and keep an exported read-only archive of your QuickBooks history for tax and audit compliance.
Self-hosting Akaunting requires technical knowledge of web servers, PHP, and MySQL databases.
If you do not have a dedicated IT team, opt for Akaunting Cloud to let their managed service handle server maintenance, security patches, and automated backups.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Can I import my historical QuickBooks invoices and bills directly into Akaunting?
Direct migration of historical transactional data is complex due to differing database schemas between QuickBooks and Akaunting. It is usually recommended to import only your current lists and opening balances, leaving historical records in exported CSV archives.
Is Akaunting really free compared to the high monthly cost of QuickBooks?
Yes, the core Akaunting software is free and open-source when self-hosted on your own server. However, you may incur optional costs if you choose Akaunting Cloud hosting or purchase premium apps from their marketplace.
How do I handle bank feeds in Akaunting after leaving QuickBooks?
Akaunting integrates with various banking providers and payment gateways depending on your region and the apps you install. You can securely connect your bank accounts to automatically sync and categorize transactions just like you did in QuickBooks.
