StreamYard vs OBS Studio (2026)

Overview

StreamYard is a browser‑based streaming platform that handles encoding, multistream, and audience interaction without any local installation. OBS Studio is a free, open‑source broadcasting application that runs on Windows, macOS, and Linux, giving users full control over encoding, sources, and output destinations. This comparison helps live‑video producers decide whether to stay with a managed SaaS solution or move to a self‑hosted, highly configurable tool. Switch if you need deeper customization, lower long‑term cost, or full data ownership.

Key Differences

  • Cost: StreamYard charges $25–$49 per month per host; OBS Studio is free (only hardware/hosting costs).
  • Data ownership: StreamYard stores recordings on its servers; OBS stores raw files locally, giving you complete ownership.
  • Setup complexity: StreamYard works out‑of‑the‑box in a browser; OBS requires installation, scene configuration, and stream‑key setup.
  • Scalability: StreamYard scales automatically to unlimited viewers but limited to its pricing tiers; OBS can stream to any number of viewers as long as your upstream bandwidth and server capacity support it.
  • Feature/Integration: StreamYard includes built‑in guest‑invitation links and on‑screen comments; OBS relies on third‑party plugins (e.g., NDI, Streamlabs) for similar functionality.

Pros and Cons

StreamYard Pros

  • No software installation; works on any modern browser.
  • Automatic multistream to Facebook, YouTube, LinkedIn, and more.
  • Built‑in branding tools (overlays, lower thirds).
  • Reliable cloud encoding; no local hardware requirements.

Cons

  • Ongoing subscription cost scales with number of hosts.
  • Recordings stored on StreamYard servers, limiting raw‑file access.
  • Limited advanced video effects compared to OBS.
  • No self‑hosting; you are dependent on StreamYard’s uptime.

OBS Studio Pros

  • Completely free and open‑source.
  • Full control over encoding settings, scene composition, and plugins.
  • Local recordings give you 100 % data ownership.
  • Works with any RTMP destination, custom servers, or CDN.

Cons

  • Steeper learning curve; requires manual scene and audio routing.
  • No native guest‑invitation link – you must use external video‑call tools.
  • No built‑in multistream; requires third‑party services or additional hardware.
  • Limitation: No built‑in cloud recording; you must allocate local storage and manage backups.

When to Choose Each

StreamYard is ideal for marketing teams, small‑to‑medium businesses, and social‑media managers who need a quick, reliable way to broadcast live events, webinars, or product demos without technical overhead. It shines when you value turnkey multistream, on‑screen comments, and a predictable monthly expense over deep customization.

OBS Studio fits production houses, tech‑savvy creators, and organizations that require granular control over video quality, custom graphics, or self‑hosted streaming infrastructure. It is best for teams that can allocate engineering time to set up scenes, manage encoding, and handle their own storage, especially when scaling to large audiences or integrating niche hardware (e.g., capture cards, NDI sources).

Migration Path

  1. Download recordings – In StreamYard, export each broadcast as an MP4 file from the “Recordings” tab.
  2. Set up OBS scenes – Create new scenes that match your StreamYard layout (e.g., host video, lower thirds, brand overlay). Import the MP4 files as Media Source items for on‑demand replay or repurposing.
  3. Configure streaming destination – Obtain the RTMP URL and stream key from your new platform (YouTube, Twitch, custom CDN) and paste them into OBS → Settings → Stream.
  4. Test locally – Run a short test stream to verify audio sync, bitrate, and overlay positioning. Adjust encoding (CBR, 4500 kbps for 1080p) as needed.
  5. Go live – Disable StreamYard, start streaming from OBS, and monitor the destination’s analytics for any latency or drop‑frame issues.

Our Take

OBS Studio is now mature enough for most live‑streaming workflows. Its 2025‑2026 release cycle added hardware‑accelerated NVENC/AMD VCE support for 4K 60 fps, a revamped audio mixer with VST plugin support, and native NDI integration, closing the gap with cloud platforms on quality and flexibility. For creators comfortable with a desktop application, OBS delivers professional‑grade results without recurring fees.

A notable technical limitation remains: OBS does not provide a built‑in, browser‑based guest‑invitation system. Teams must rely on external video‑call services (Zoom, Google Meet) and capture those windows, which adds latency and extra steps. This can be a deal‑breaker for non‑technical hosts who need seamless guest onboarding.

Recommendation: Switch to OBS if you have at least one dedicated technical person, need full data ownership, or want to eliminate per‑host subscription costs. Stay with StreamYard if your team prioritizes ease of use, instant multistream, and minimal setup time, especially when guest management is a core requirement.

Who Should Switch

  • Small production teams (2‑5 people) that already edit video locally and don’t need built‑in guest links.
  • Organizations with a strict data‑privacy policy requiring all recordings to stay on‑premises.
  • Tech startups that want to cut recurring SaaS costs for 10+ regular hosts.
  • Live‑gaming channels that need low‑latency custom overlays and hardware‑accelerated encoding.
  • NGOs or educational institutions with limited budgets but existing IT staff to maintain a streaming server.

FAQ

Does OBS Studio support multistream to multiple platforms at once?

Not natively. OBS can stream to a single RTMP endpoint; to multistream you need a third‑party service (Restream, StreamYard’s own multistream) or a self‑hosted solution like Nginx‑RTMP.

Can I record directly to the cloud with OBS Studio?

OBS records locally to MP4, MKV, or FLV. Cloud storage requires a separate upload step or a plugin that pushes files to services such as AWS S3 or Google Drive.

Is there a free version of StreamYard?

Yes, StreamYard offers a free tier with limited branding, a maximum of 20 minutes per broadcast, and up to 10 participants. Advanced features require a paid plan.

Will switching to OBS affect my existing audience numbers?

No. Audience size depends on the destination platform (YouTube, Twitch, etc.). OBS simply changes how you send the video to those platforms; viewer counts remain unchanged.

Does OBS Studio run on mobile devices?

OBS Studio is desktop‑only. There is a separate OBS‑mobile app for Android/iOS, but it is a limited companion and not a full replacement for the desktop version.
