Zoom vs Jitsi Meet (2026)

Overview

Zoom and Jitsi Meet are prominent video conferencing platforms used for remote collaboration, team meetings, and webinars. This comparison targets IT administrators, engineering leads, and cost-conscious organizations evaluating proprietary cloud infrastructure against open-source alternatives. Organizations typically switch from Zoom to Jitsi Meet to eliminate per-user subscription fees, regain complete control over data routing, and adhere to strict internal privacy mandates.

Key Differences

  • Cost: Zoom operates on a tiered SaaS subscription model with paid per-user licensing, whereas Jitsi Meet is entirely free and open-source software.
  • Data Ownership: Zoom routes and stores call data on proprietary cloud servers, while Jitsi Meet allows organizations to deploy and host the entire stack on private infrastructure.
  • Setup Complexity: Zoom requires no administrative setup beyond account creation, whereas Jitsi Meet requires Linux system administration expertise for self-hosting and ongoing server maintenance.
  • Scalability: Zoom scales effortlessly to thousands of participants via elastic cloud infrastructure, while Jitsi Meet's capacity is strictly bound to the hardware resources of the hosting server and network bandwidth.
  • Feature Integration: Zoom includes native cloud recording and built-in transcriptions, whereas Jitsi Meet relies on external integrations like Jigasi for SIP gateway support and local recording setups.

Pros and Cons

Zoom

Pros:
  • Highly reliable cloud infrastructure with minimal latency globally.
  • Extensive library of native third-party integrations (Slack, Google Workspace, Salesforce).
  • User-friendly interface requiring zero technical onboarding for participants.
  • Advanced features like automated AI transcription and breakout rooms out of the box.

Cons:

  • Escalating per-user subscription costs for large teams.
  • Proprietary architecture prevents strict on-premises data residency compliance.
  • Account management and user provisioning require paid administrative tiers.
  • Free tier enforces a hard 40-minute time limit on group meetings.

Jitsi Meet

Pros:
  • 100% free and open-source with no per-user licensing fees.
  • Complete data sovereignty via self-hosted deployments.
  • No mandatory user accounts or sign-ups required to join a meeting.
  • Lightweight web client that operates entirely in-browser without required installations.

Cons:

  • Self-hosting requires dedicated DevOps resources for maintenance and scaling.
  • Audio and video quality degrade rapidly if the host server lacks sufficient bandwidth or CPU.
  • Lacks native cloud recording, requiring complex third-party configurations like Jibri.
  • Limited native enterprise integrations compared to commercial suites.

When to Choose Each

Choose Zoom if you manage a non-technical organization that requires out-of-the-box reliability, native AI meeting summaries, complex breakout room management, and seamless integrations with enterprise productivity suites without allocating internal DevOps resources.

Choose Jitsi Meet if you operate a technical team with dedicated systems administration resources, require absolute control over data privacy and routing, and need to host unlimited-duration video calls without per-seat software licensing overhead.

Migration Path

  1. Provision a dedicated Linux server (Ubuntu 22.04 LTS recommended) with adequate CPU, RAM, and network bandwidth to handle your peak concurrent user load.
  2. Install the Jitsi Meet stack using the official Debian/Ubuntu package repositories and configure your domain DNS records.
  3. Configure SSL/TLS certificates using Let's Encrypt to secure all media streams and web traffic.
  4. Export user directories or create communication templates informing internal teams of the new Jitsi server URL.
  5. Decommission paid Zoom user accounts and redirect all internal calendar invites to the newly deployed Jitsi domain.

Our Take

Jitsi Meet is production-ready for organizations that possess the internal Linux administration skills required to deploy, monitor, and scale WebRTC infrastructure. For teams with fewer than 50 users who require zero maintenance overhead, self-hosting Jitsi rarely justifies the operational friction compared to commercial alternatives. However, for privacy-first organizations or large enterprises seeking to eliminate runaway SaaS licensing fees, Jitsi provides a robust, highly capable foundation.

One specific technical limitation worth knowing before switching is Jitsi's handling of recording. Unlike Zoom's seamless cloud recording, Jitsi requires deploying an additional component called Jibri (Jitsi Broadcasting Infrastructure), which runs headless instances of Chrome to record sessions. This process consumes heavy CPU and RAM per concurrent recording and introduces significant configuration complexity.

The recommendation is clear: technical teams, privacy-conscious institutions, and organizations with existing DevOps capacity should switch to Jitsi Meet to slash software overhead and secure their data. Non-technical businesses, sales-heavy organizations dependent on CRM integrations, and teams lacking system administration staff should remain on Zoom.

Who Should Switch

  • Engineering-led startups with under 100 employees who do not need proprietary CRM integrations and want to eliminate monthly SaaS overhead.
  • Healthcare and legal practices with strict data residency mandates that do not need automated AI transcription services stored on third-party servers.
  • Educational institutions with internal IT departments that do not need Zoom's commercial license tiers to host remote classrooms.
  • Non-profit organizations operating on tight budgets with fewer than 200 daily users who do not need native cloud recording infrastructure.
  • Open-source software projects and developer communities that do not need enterprise user management dashboards.

FAQ

Does Jitsi Meet have a time limit on meetings like the free version of Zoom?

No, Jitsi Meet has no built-in time limits for meetings. You can host calls that last for hours without interruption, regardless of whether you use the public community server or a self-hosted instance.

Can I record meetings on Jitsi Meet?

Yes, but recording requires a separate service called Jibri configured on your self-hosted instance, or integrating with a cloud storage provider like Dropbox. It does not work out of the box with a single click on standard community servers.

Is Jitsi Meet secure and encrypted?

Yes, Jitsi Meet uses DTLS-SRTP for media encryption in transit between participants and the server. However, end-to-end encryption (E2EE) is experimental and requires all participants to use modern browsers supporting specific WebRTC standards.

Do meeting participants need to create an account to use Jitsi Meet?

No, participants do not need an account, password, or software installation to join a meeting. They simply click the room link or enter the room name in their web browser to join instantly.

How many participants can join a single Jitsi Meet room?

While theoretically limited only by server hardware and bandwidth, Jitsi officially recommends a maximum of 35 participants per room for optimal performance on standard deployments. Exceeding this number without advanced configuration will severely degrade video and audio quality.
