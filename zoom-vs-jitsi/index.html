Zoom vs Jitsi Meet (2026)

Overview

Zoom and Jitsi Meet are web conferencing platforms used for remote team communication, client meetings, and virtual classrooms. This comparison benefits IT administrators, privacy-conscious organizations, and budget-driven engineering teams evaluating cloud-proprietary video conferencing against open-source infrastructure. The core use case for both is real-time video and audio collaboration, with teams typically switching from Zoom to Jitsi Meet to eliminate per-user software licensing costs and retain complete ownership of their meeting data.

Key Differences

  • Cost: Zoom operates on a tiered SaaS pricing model with paid per-user monthly subscriptions, whereas Jitsi Meet is completely free and open-source software with no per-seat licensing fees.
  • Data Ownership: Zoom processes meeting metadata and media streams through its proprietary cloud infrastructure and third-party data centers, while self-hosted Jitsi Meet keeps all media, logs, and metadata entirely within your private infrastructure.
  • Setup Complexity: Zoom offers instant out-of-the-box deployment requiring only an account creation, whereas self-hosting Jitsi Meet requires Linux server administration, DNS configuration, SSL certificate management, and WebRTC network tuning (STUN/TURN).
  • Scalability: Zoom provides elastic cloud scalability supporting thousands of participants per meeting out of the box, while Jitsi Meet server capacity is strictly bound to the CPU, RAM, and bandwidth limits of your underlying host or Jitsi Videobridge (JVB) cluster.
  • Ecosystem Integration: Zoom features a massive marketplace with native integrations for Salesforce, Slack, and hundreds of productivity tools, whereas Jitsi Meet relies primarily on lighter web-embedded SDKs, Matrix/Element integrations, and custom API implementations.

Pros and Cons

Zoom

Pros:
  • Zero infrastructure setup or maintenance required
  • Highly reliable global network with consistent audio/video quality
  • Extensive third-party app marketplace and calendar integrations
  • Advanced participant controls, breakout rooms, and live transcription

Cons:

  • Escalating per-user monthly subscription costs for professional features
  • Proprietary software prevents deep code customization or local auditing
  • Free tier enforces a strict 40-minute time limit on group meetings
  • Data routing depends on external cloud providers

Jitsi Meet

Pros:
  • 100% free and open-source under the Apache 2.0 license
  • Complete control over data privacy, compliance, and server geography
  • No account or registration required for participants to join a call
  • Easy embedding into web and mobile apps via the Jitsi Meet API

Cons:

  • Requires dedicated technical resources to deploy, scale, and maintain
  • Lacks native out-of-the-box integrations for enterprise SaaS suites
  • Advanced features like cloud recording require complex manual configuration
  • Performance depends heavily on self-hosted server hardware and bandwidth

When to Choose Each

Zoom is best suited for businesses, educational institutions, and non-technical teams of any size that require reliable, zero-maintenance video conferencing with deep integrations into existing enterprise software like Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, and Slack.

Jitsi Meet is ideal for privacy-focused organizations, development teams, and budget-conscious enterprises with internal IT resources who want to deploy a custom, self-hosted video conferencing solution without recurring per-user licensing fees or third-party data tracking.

Migration Path

  1. Audit your current Zoom usage, identifying required administrative controls, user licenses, and active calendar integrations.
  2. Provision a dedicated Linux cloud server (Ubuntu 22.04 LTS recommended) with adequate CPU, RAM, and network bandwidth.
  3. Install Jitsi Meet on your server using official Debian/Ubuntu packages and configure your DNS records and Let's Encrypt SSL certificates.
  4. Export your user directory from Zoom as a CSV file (Settings > Account Management > Users) if migrating user profiles for internal authentication integrations.
  5. Update your organization's meeting links, calendar templates, and employee bookmarks to point to your new self-hosted Jitsi domain.

Our Take

Jitsi Meet is production-ready for tech-savvy teams and organizations with dedicated IT resources, but it remains overly complex for non-technical office environments. While the software is remarkably stable for standard video calls, running it reliably at scale requires active server monitoring, network tuning, and proactive patch management.

One specific technical limitation worth knowing before switching is that Jitsi Meet relies heavily on a selective forwarding architecture (JVB) that can saturate server bandwidth quickly. Unlike Zoom's distributed cloud routing, a single self-hosted Jitsi instance channels all video streams through your server, meaning your network uplink speed directly caps your maximum concurrent participant capacity.

Organizations with internal Linux administration skills and strict data residency requirements should switch immediately to eliminate software licensing costs. Conversely, teams lacking dedicated IT staff who rely on out-of-the-box integrations should not switch, as the operational overhead of self-hosting will quickly outweigh any financial savings.

Who Should Switch

  • Mid-sized engineering teams of 20 to 100 developers who already manage cloud infrastructure and do not need proprietary SaaS integrations.
  • Healthcare providers and legal practices with strict data privacy mandates who cannot route metadata through third-party proprietary clouds.
  • Bootstrapped startups and open-source communities with fewer than 50 members who want to eliminate recurring per-user software licensing costs.
  • Educational institutions wanting to embed secure video rooms directly into a custom learning management system without paying enterprise seat fees.
  • Privacy-first NGOs and activist groups operating under strict threat models who require absolute ownership of their communication logs.

FAQ

Can Jitsi Meet replace Zoom for large webinars?

Jitsi Meet is optimized for multi-party video conferencing rather than massive unidirectional webinars. While you can stream Jitsi meetings via YouTube integration, it lacks native webinar controls like attendee registration, hand-raising queues, and scaled Q&A panels.

Does Jitsi Meet have a free tier like Zoom?

Jitsi Meet is entirely free and open-source with no artificial time limits, participant caps, or locked features. However, running it involves paying for your own server hosting and bandwidth if you choose to self-host.

Is Jitsi Meet secure and encrypted?

Jitsi Meet supports end-to-end encryption (E2EE) for peer-to-peer connections and uses DTLS-SRTP for media encryption in bridged mode. When self-hosted, your organization retains complete cryptographic control over the infrastructure.

Do participants need to create an account to join a Jitsi meeting?

No, participants can join a Jitsi Meet session instantly via a web browser or mobile app simply by clicking a shared URL. No registration or software installation is strictly required.

Can I record meetings on Jitsi Meet?

Yes, Jitsi Meet supports meeting recording, but it requires configuring an external Jibri (Jitsi Broadcasting Infrastructure) container alongside your server. It does not offer automatic cloud recording out of the box like Zoom.
