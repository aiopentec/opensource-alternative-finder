📦 Migration Guide

How to Migrate from Notion to Obsidian

💰 Save $8-$20/user/month/month 🔓 Own your data 🖥️ Setup: Very Easy (~2 mins) 📅 August 28, 2026

Notion is a powerful all‑in‑one workspace, but its subscription pricing ($8‑$20 per user per month) and cloud‑only storage can become a pain point for individuals or teams that need offline access, granular version control, or complete data ownership. Obsidian, by contrast, stores every note as a plain‑text markdown file on your local drive, is free for personal use, and offers a thriving plugin ecosystem that can replicate many of Notion’s database and collaboration features without recurring fees. When a workflow starts to rely heavily on backlinks, rapid note‑taking, or when the cost of scaling Notion users outweighs its convenience, many users find the switch to Obsidian both financially and technically appealing. Additionally, privacy‑focused professionals appreciate that Obsidian never syncs your data unless you enable a third‑party service, giving you full control over backups and encryption. This guide walks you through the practical steps of moving your content, setting up a shared vault for teams, and handling the quirks that arise when translating Notion’s block‑based pages into Obsidian’s markdown‑centric vault.

🗺️ Migration Steps

1
Export Notion pages
In Notion, select the workspace or individual pages you want to move, click ‘Export’, and choose the Markdown + CSV format with sub‑pages included. The export will generate a zip file containing a folder hierarchy that mirrors your Notion page tree. Download the zip to a location where you can safely unpack it for the next step.
2
Unpack and clean up files
Extract the zip and review the folder structure; Notion often creates extra ‘Untitled’ folders and duplicate markdown files for embedded databases. Rename ambiguous files, delete empty folders, and move any attached images from the ‘assets’ sub‑folders into a central ‘media’ directory that you will later point to from Obsidian.
3
Create an Obsidian vault
Open Obsidian and click ‘Create new vault’, giving it a name that reflects your project or team. Choose the folder where you unpacked the Notion export as the vault location, or create a fresh folder and copy the cleaned markdown files into it. This vault will become the single source of truth for all future notes.
4
Import markdown files
Obsidian automatically indexes any *.md files placed in the vault, so once the files are copied, they appear in the sidebar as a nested graph. Verify that internal links (e.g., [[Page Name]]) still resolve; Notion’s export often uses plain URLs that need to be manually converted to Obsidian’s wiki‑link syntax.
5
Recreate databases and views
Notion tables export as CSV files; import each CSV into Obsidian using the Dataview or Table plugins, which let you treat CSV rows as linked notes or generate dynamic tables. For more complex relational databases, consider the ‘Obsidian‑Kanban’ or ‘Meta‑Bind’ plugins to simulate board views and linked properties.
6
Team onboarding and final cutover
If you work with a team, share the vault via a cloud‑sync service such as Obsidian Sync, Git, or a shared network drive. Conduct a short training session to show how backlinks, tags, and plugins replace Notion’s relational blocks. Once everyone confirms that critical pages load correctly, archive the Notion workspace and switch daily operations to the Obsidian vault.

⚠️ Common Challenges & How to Avoid Them

Relational links break during export
After importing, run a global search for the original Notion URLs and replace them with Obsidian wiki‑links using a find‑and‑replace script or the Bulk Rename plugin.
Embedded media files are scattered
Consolidate all images and PDFs into a single ‘media’ folder and update the markdown image paths to point to that folder; the Obsidian ‘Attachment Folder Path’ setting can automate this for new notes.
Loss of Notion comments and mentions
Comments aren’t exported, so copy any essential discussion into a dedicated “Comments” section at the bottom of each note before deleting the Notion page.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Can I keep Notion pages synced with Obsidian after migration?
There is no native two‑way sync; you can periodically re‑export from Notion and overwrite the vault, but most users treat the Obsidian vault as the primary source and retire the Notion workspace.
How do I migrate Notion databases to Obsidian?
Export each database as a CSV, then use plugins like Dataview or Table to render the CSV as linked notes or dynamic tables. You may need to create separate note files for rows that you want to reference individually.
Is there a way to preserve Notion comments and mentions in Obsidian?
Comments are not part of the markdown export, so they must be copied manually. You can add them as blockquotes or a “Comments” heading inside the corresponding Obsidian note to retain the context.
