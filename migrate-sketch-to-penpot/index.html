📦 Migration Guide

How to Migrate from Sketch to Penpot

💰 Save $9-$20/user/month/month 🔓 Own your data 🖥️ Setup: Easy (~30 mins) 📅 August 29, 2026

Migrating from Sketch to Penpot represents a strategic shift from a macOS-centric, subscription-based design environment to a web-native, open-source platform that eliminates per-user licensing fees. Teams often make this move to break free from Mac hardware locks, streamline developer handoff using CSS-native flexbox and grid layouts, and embrace truly open standards where design files are stored transparently in SVG.

While Sketch has long been a staple of the Mac ecosystem with its mature plugin architecture and native performance, Penpot offers modern collaborative capabilities that rival cloud-first competitors without sacrificing user freedom or data privacy. You should consider migrating when your organization expands to cross-platform operating systems, when you want to self-host your design infrastructure for compliance, or when you are looking to eliminate recurring design software overhead as your team scales.

Executing this transition successfully requires understanding how Sketch symbols and text styles map to Penpot components and typography tokens. Although the underlying paradigms share similarities, bridging the gap between macOS desktop workflows and browser-based SVG rendering demands a structured approach to auditing libraries, cleaning up legacy artboards, and retraining team members on Penpot layout engines.

🗺️ Migration Steps

1
Audit and Clean Your Sketch Libraries
Before exporting, open your Sketch files and clean up unused symbols, orphan text styles, and cluttered layers. Consolidate your design tokens so that color palettes and typography scales translate cleanly without generating redundant styles in Penpot.
2
Export Assets and Prepare .sketch Files
Ensure your Sketch documents are saved locally in the standard .sketch format. While Penpot features native SVG import capabilities, keeping your source files organized ensures that artboards, groups, and bitmap assets retain their hierarchy during conversion.
3
Set Up Your Penpot Workspace
Create a Penpot account or provision your self-hosted instance, then configure your team spaces and permission structures. Recreate your global design tokens—such as color palettes and grid parameters—in Penpot's dashboard before importing actual design files.
4
Import Files into Penpot
Use Penpot's import functionality to upload your prepared .sketch files directly into your designated workspaces. Review the imported artboards immediately to verify that fonts, vector paths, and nested component structures rendered correctly.
5
Rebuild Complex Components and Overrides
Because Sketch symbols and Penpot components handle overrides differently, you will need to manually inspect complex UI kits. Re-establish component variants, interactive states, and responsive constraints using Penpot's CSS-based layout tools.
6
Onboard Team and Execute Cutover
Conduct team workshops focusing on Penpot's web-native interface, comment threads, and developer inspection tools. Once the team is comfortable, archive your legacy Sketch files and designate Penpot as the single source of truth for all active product design.

⚠️ Common Challenges & How to Avoid Them

Sketch plugins and custom data generators do not transfer over to Penpot.
Identify critical workflow plugins in Sketch and find native Penpot alternatives or build custom scripts using Penpot's open API.
Complex symbol overrides and nested symbol structures may break or flatten during import.
Rebuild heavily nested components natively inside Penpot using its robust component system and flexible layout rules.
Proprietary or local macOS system fonts used in Sketch may not render identically in a browser-based tool.
Upload missing typography files to Penpot's shared workspace font library or standardize on web-safe Google Fonts.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Can I open .sketch files directly in Penpot?
Yes, Penpot supports importing standard .sketch files. However, due to differences in rendering engines and layout models, you should expect to spend some time adjusting complex components, text styles, and constraints after the import is complete.
How does Penpot handle layout compared to Sketch?
Sketch relies heavily on grouped layers and third-party plugins for layout, whereas Penpot is built entirely around web standards using Flexbox and CSS Grid. This means your designs translate much more naturally into production-ready code for developers.
Is Penpot really free, and can I host it on our own servers?
Penpot is open-source and free to use on their cloud infrastructure, but it also offers a self-hosted Docker version. This makes it ideal for organizations with strict data governance policies who want complete control over their design infrastructure without paying per-seat fees.
