QuickBooks vs Akaunting (2026)

Overview

QuickBooks and Akaunting are financial management platforms designed to handle invoicing, expense tracking, and basic accounting workflows for businesses. This comparison is intended for small business owners, IT administrators, and finance leads evaluating whether to move from a dominant proprietary SaaS model to an open-source, self-hosted alternative. The core use case for both is day-to-day bookkeeping, while the primary motivation for switching is escaping recurring per-user subscription fees in favor of complete data ownership.

Key Differences

  • Cost Structure: QuickBooks charges a recurring monthly subscription fee ranging from $30 to over $200 per month depending on tier, whereas Akaunting is fundamentally free and open-source when self-hosted on your own infrastructure.
  • Data Ownership: QuickBooks stores all financial data on proprietary Intuit-controlled cloud servers, while Akaunting allows self-hosted deployments where you retain 100% control over your database and server environment.
  • Setup Complexity: QuickBooks offers an out-of-the-box hosted experience that requires zero infrastructure setup, whereas self-hosting Akaunting requires technical competency in web server administration, PHP, MySQL, and SSL certificate management.
  • Scalability & User Limits: QuickBooks tiers restrict the maximum number of users per account (e.g., up to 25 users on Advanced), whereas self-hosted Akaunting imposes no software limits on user counts, keeping licensing costs at zero regardless of headcount.
  • Feature and Integration Ecosystem: QuickBooks provides native, deep integrations with hundreds of proprietary third-party fintech and payroll tools, whereas Akaunting relies on its own app store modules, many of which require individual paid licenses for advanced functionality.

Pros and Cons

QuickBooks

Pros:
  • Zero infrastructure management or server maintenance required
  • Comprehensive, native integrations with major payroll, banking, and tax platforms
  • Extensive library of certified public accountants (CPAs) familiar with the platform
  • Robust automated bank feeds with high compatibility rates

Cons:

  • High and escalating monthly subscription costs
  • Per-user limits force expensive tier upgrades
  • Data is locked into a proprietary cloud with limited export options
  • Pricing changes are unilaterally imposed by Intuit

Akaunting

Pros:
  • Complete control over financial data and hosting environment
  • Zero licensing or per-user fees when self-hosted
  • Clean, modern, modular interface built on Laravel
  • Open-source codebase allows custom code modifications

Cons:

  • Requires technical resources for server deployment, backups, and security patching
  • Smaller third-party integration ecosystem compared to QuickBooks
  • Many advanced modules and payment gateway integrations require separate paid licenses
  • Customer support relies on community forums unless a paid support plan is purchased

When to Choose Each

Choose QuickBooks if you operate a growing small-to-medium business that requires immediate, out-of-the-box integrations with US banking systems, automated payroll, and a platform that your external CPA already uses daily without technical overhead.

Choose Akaunting if you are a tech-savvy business, developer, or privacy-conscious organization with the internal infrastructure to self-host, and you want to eliminate recurring per-user software licensing fees while retaining absolute ownership of your financial records.

Migration Path

  1. Export Data from QuickBooks: Export your Chart of Accounts, Customers, Vendors, and Products/Services as CSV files using the built-in QuickBooks export reporting tools.
  2. Provision the Server: Set up a production-ready Linux server running PHP and MySQL, and complete the standard self-hosted installation of Akaunting.
  3. Configure Settings: Set up your company profile, currency, tax rates, and base accounting preferences within Akaunting to match your former QuickBooks setup.
  4. Import via Modules: Use the Akaunting Import tool or compatible CSV import apps from the Akaunting App Store to map and upload your exported customer, vendor, and item lists.
  5. Reconcile Opening Balances: Run a final trial balance report in QuickBooks as of your cutover date, and manually enter opening journal entries and account balances into Akaunting to verify accuracy.

Our Take

Akaunting is production-ready for small businesses and freelancers with basic invoicing and expense-tracking needs, but growing enterprises may find its module ecosystem less polished than legacy SaaS platforms. Because it relies heavily on community-contributed or paid third-party add-ons, users must be prepared to manage plugin compatibility during core software updates. One specific technical limitation worth knowing before switching is that Akaunting lacks native, automated US payroll processing; you will need to handle payroll entirely outside the system or integrate a separate external provider. Ultimately, we recommend switching to Akaunting if you have dedicated IT resources, zero tolerance for recurring per-user software fees, and a strict mandate for on-premise data control. Conversely, businesses that rely on turn-key payroll, deep automated bank feeds, and zero-maintenance infrastructure should stay with QuickBooks.

Who Should Switch

  • Freelancers and solo consultants managing fewer than 5 clients who do not need automated US payroll or advanced inventory tracking.
  • Small digital agencies with 5 to 15 team members who want to eliminate escalating per-user monthly SaaS fees.
  • International businesses operating outside the US tax ecosystem that do not require QuickBooks' native US-centric compliance features.
  • Tech-focused startups with internal DevOps capabilities who prioritize absolute data ownership and self-hosting over turnkey convenience.
  • Non-profit organizations with strict data residency mandates that prohibit storing financial records on third-party proprietary cloud servers.

FAQ

Is Akaunting completely free?

The core self-hosted version of Akaunting is open-source and free to download and use without licensing fees. However, you will incur costs for server hosting, SSL certificates, and optional paid extensions or payment gateway modules from their app store.

Can Akaunting handle automatic bank feeds like QuickBooks?

Yes, Akaunting supports bank integrations through various banking apps available in its store, though availability depends heavily on your geographic region and the specific financial institutions you use.

Does Akaunting support multi-currency accounting?

Yes, Akaunting features built-in multi-currency support, allowing you to invoice clients, track expenses, and manage accounts in different currencies with automated exchange rate updates.

Is my financial data secure on Akaunting?

When self-hosting, security is entirely your responsibility. You must secure your server, configure firewalls, enforce strong authentication, and maintain regular database backups to protect your financial records.

Can I migrate my historical transaction data from QuickBooks?

You can export lists like customers, vendors, and charts of accounts via CSV, but importing historical transaction lines often requires manual journal entries or specialized third-party migration scripts due to differing database schemas.
