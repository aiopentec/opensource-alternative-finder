QuickBooks vs Akaunting (2026)

Overview

QuickBooks is a proprietary cloud-based accounting platform designed for small-to-medium businesses that require out-of-the-box bank feeds, extensive third-party integrations, and automated tax compliance. Akaunting is an open-source, modular financial software that can be self-hosted or run via their cloud service, targeting users who prioritize data ownership and zero software licensing costs. This comparison evaluates both tools for businesses deciding between an established SaaS ecosystem and a customizable, self-hosted alternative.

Key Differences

  • Cost: QuickBooks operates on a tiered monthly SaaS subscription ranging from $30 to $200+ per month, whereas Akaunting's self-hosted version is completely free under the GPL v3 license.
  • Data Ownership: QuickBooks stores all financial data on proprietary Intuit servers in the cloud, while self-hosted Akaunting gives you 100% control over your database and server infrastructure.
  • Setup Complexity: QuickBooks requires zero technical infrastructure setup beyond creating an account, while self-hosted Akaunting requires provisioning a web server, configuring a database (MySQL/PostgreSQL), and managing PHP environments.
  • Scalability: QuickBooks scales pricing linearly per user and transaction volume tiers, whereas self-hosted Akaunting charges zero per-user fees, with scaling limited only by your underlying server hardware.
  • Feature Integration: QuickBooks includes native US/international tax tables and automated bank feeds out of the box, whereas Akaunting relies on modular add-ons (many paid) from its app store to achieve similar specialized functionality.

Pros and Cons

QuickBooks

  • Pros:
* Native, automated bank feeds with major global financial institutions. * Comprehensive app marketplace with thousands of pre-built integrations. * Built-in payroll and direct tax filing capabilities for multiple jurisdictions. * Dedicated 24/7 customer support channels included in subscription tiers.
  • Cons:
* High and recurring monthly subscription costs that scale aggressively with user count. * Vendor lock-in with closed-source proprietary data formats. * Frequent UI/UX changes and forced workflow updates by Intuit. * Data privacy is subject to Intuit's corporate privacy policies and cloud storage.

Akaunting

  • Pros:
* Complete data sovereignty through self-hosting options. * No per-user licensing fees, making multi-user access free. * Open-source codebase (GPL v3) allows for deep customization and auditing. * Clean, modern modular interface built on Laravel.
  • Cons:
* Requires internal IT resources or technical knowledge to install, update, and secure. * Many advanced features, payment gateways, and integrations require purchasing paid add-ons. * Community support only; official priority support requires a paid cloud or support plan. * Bank feed integrations depend on third-party connectors which may require separate subscriptions.

When to Choose Each

Choose QuickBooks if your business requires out-of-the-box automated bank reconciliations, direct payroll tax filing, and a hands-off SaaS experience backed by dedicated customer support. It fits small to mid-sized businesses, agencies, and e-commerce operations that rely on a vast ecosystem of third-party SaaS integrations and do not have dedicated IT staff to manage server infrastructure.

Choose Akaunting if you are an organization, freelancer, or non-profit with technical capabilities who wants complete ownership of your financial data without ongoing per-user subscription fees. It is ideal for teams comfortable with self-hosting LAMP/LEMP stacks who need core invoicing, expense tracking, and client management without paying enterprise SaaS premiums.

Migration Path

  1. Export your master data (Chart of Accounts, Customers, Vendors, Products) from QuickBooks into CSV format using the built-in report export tools.
  2. Set up your target environment by installing self-hosted Akaunting on your web server and completing the initial admin configuration.
  3. Install the data import module within Akaunting or use CSV import tools to map and upload your exported QuickBooks customers, vendors, and opening balances.
  4. Manually verify historical financial statements, account balances, and open invoices in Akaunting against your QuickBooks records to ensure data integrity.

Our Take

Evaluating Akaunting for production use requires a realistic look at your internal technical capabilities. For most standard business users without dedicated IT staff, the self-hosted version of Akaunting demands too much maintenance regarding server security, database backups, and software updates. However, for organizations with sysadmin resources, it provides a remarkably stable and clean alternative to expensive SaaS platforms.

The primary technical limitation to know before switching is bank integration reliability. Unlike QuickBooks, which maintains direct, automated API pipelines with thousands of global financial institutions, Akaunting's bank feeds rely on third-party aggregation modules that frequently require separate paid subscriptions and occasional manual troubleshooting.

We recommend QuickBooks for growing businesses that need turnkey compliance, automated payroll, and hands-off reliability, and are willing to absorb rising subscription costs. You should switch to Akaunting if you run a small-to-medium team, have the technical capacity to self-host, and want to eliminate recurring per-user software fees while keeping your financial database entirely on your own servers.

Who Should Switch

  • Freelancers and solo consultants managing fewer than 50 clients who do not need advanced inventory management or payroll automation.
  • Small digital agencies with 5 to 15 team members who do not need automated tax filing and want to eliminate per-user software licensing fees.
  • Non-profit organizations operating on tight budgets with internal IT support who do not need multi-currency payroll integrations.
  • Development shops and tech-forward startups that prefer self-hosted infrastructure and do not need native QuickBooks-specific extensions.
  • Small businesses located outside the US/UK who struggle with QuickBooks' regional pricing models and do not need domestic automated tax tables.

FAQ

Is Akaunting really 100% free?

The self-hosted version of Akaunting is free and open-source under the GPL v3 license, meaning you pay zero software licensing fees. However, you will incur costs for your own server hosting, domain, SSL certificates, and optionally for premium add-ons or cloud-hosted plans.

Can Akaunting handle payroll like QuickBooks?

Not natively out of the box. While QuickBooks includes built-in payroll and tax filing for various regions, Akaunting relies on third-party extensions or manual journal entries to handle employee payroll calculations and disbursements.

How difficult is it to self-host Akaunting?

If you have experience managing a standard web server running PHP and MySQL (such as an Ubuntu VPS with Nginx or Apache), setting up Akaunting is straightforward and similar to installing WordPress. If you have no command-line or server administration experience, the self-hosted setup will present a steep learning curve.

Does Akaunting support automated bank feeds?

Yes, but not natively in the base free download for all regions. You must install banking modules, which often rely on third-party open-banking aggregators that may require paid subscriptions depending on your geographic location.

Can I migrate my historical transactions from QuickBooks?

You can migrate core master data like chart of accounts, customers, vendors, and products via CSV imports. However, bringing over complete historical transaction-level data requires manual data formatting or specialized third-party migration scripts, as there is no direct one-click API migration tool between the two platforms.
