📦 Migration Guide

How to Migrate from Discord to Element

💰 Save Free / $9.99/month/month 🔓 Own your data 🖥️ Setup: Moderate (~2 hours) 📅 August 25, 2026

Migrating from Discord to Element is a deliberate move away from proprietary walled gardens toward decentralized, end-to-end encrypted collaboration. Organizations and communities typically make this switch when data ownership, privacy regulations, and compliance become non-negotiable priorities. While Discord relies on centralized cloud servers controlled by a single corporation—logging message histories and metadata—Element is built on Matrix, an open-source, federated protocol that puts you in complete control of where your data lives.

Timing this migration usually coincides with a growing need for enterprise-grade security, such as HIPAA or GDPR compliance, or a desire to escape Discord’s increasingly aggressive monetization of basic features like custom emojis and larger file uploads. Transitioning from Discord servers and channels to Element spaces and rooms requires careful planning, as the underlying architecture is entirely different. However, the payoff is a resilient, transparent communication ecosystem where your community or team truly owns its digital infrastructure without vendor lock-in.

🗺️ Migration Steps

1
Audit and Export Discord Content
Begin by cataloging your existing Discord channels, roles, and permission structures. Because Discord does not offer a native tool to directly export active servers into Matrix-compatible formats, use open-source Discord chat exporters to archive historical text logs, pins, and media attachments for reference.
2
Provision Your Element Environment
Decide whether to use Element Enterprise hosted infrastructure or self-host your own Matrix homeserver (such as Synapse or Dendrite) to meet your data residency requirements. Once your homeserver is operational, download the Element desktop or mobile clients and set up your admin accounts.
3
Recreate Structure with Spaces and Rooms
Translate Discord’s server and channel hierarchy into Element's architecture by creating a Space to represent your Discord server. Inside that Space, build individual rooms that mirror your text and voice channels, and configure default end-to-end encryption for sensitive discussions.
4
Bridge Ongoing Discord Traffic
To ease the transition for your community, deploy an open-source Matrix-Discord bridge like appservice-discord. This allows messages to sync bidirectionally between your legacy Discord channels and your new Element rooms, ensuring no communication is lost during the overlap phase.
5
Onboard Users and Assign Roles
Invite your community members to create Matrix IDs on your homeserver and guide them through Element's cross-signing verification process. Map your former Discord administrative roles and permissions to Element power levels to maintain proper moderation control.
6
Execute the Final Cutover
Once your user base is active on Element and comfortable with the interface, shut down the Discord bridge and archive your old Discord server. Announce the official cutover date so all participants direct their communication exclusively through your new Element spaces.

⚠️ Common Challenges & How to Avoid Them

Discord's rich webhook ecosystem and custom bots do not work natively in Element.
Rewrite or replace your Discord bots with Matrix-compatible equivalents, utilizing Matrix webhooks and appservices which offer robust integration capabilities.
Users accustomed to Discord's casual voice and video chat may find Element's calling features unfamiliar.
Integrate a dedicated WebRTC conferencing solution like Jitsi Meet directly into your Element rooms to provide a seamless, high-capacity voice and video experience.
End-to-end encryption key management can confuse non-technical community members during initial login.
Enable Element's cryptographic cross-signing and secure key backup features during user onboarding so keys sync automatically across new devices without manual file exports.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Can I automatically import my old Discord message history directly into Element?
There is no automated native button to import Discord history directly into Matrix. However, you can use third-party chat exporters to save your logs as HTML or JSON files for archival purposes, or use a bridge to sync forward-looking messages from the moment you switch.
How does Element handle voice and video channels compared to Discord?
While Discord has built-in, always-on voice channels, Element handles voice and video through integrated widgets like Jitsi Meet or native Matrix VoIP calls. Users simply click a call button inside a specific room to spin up an encrypted audio or video conference.
Is Element really free to use like Discord?
Element's core client software and the Matrix protocol are completely free and open-source. If you choose to self-host your own homeserver, you only pay for your server hosting costs, whereas Element's managed hosting and enterprise tiers offer paid plans with advanced administrative support.
